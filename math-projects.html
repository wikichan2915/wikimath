Approximating the square root of 2

Investigate the fractions 1/1, 3/2, 7/5, 17/12, and so on. Their values get closer to √2. The numerators and denominators come from the Pell sequence, not the Fibonacci sequence, though both are built from simple recurrences.

Questions to explore: How quickly do the fractions approach √2? Can you find a recurrence that generates them? What happens to the error at each step?

Possible connections: A4 paper keeps the same aspect ratio, √2, when folded in half. Representing the recurrence with a matrix can reveal how each pair is generated; the binomial theorem offers another way to study expressions related to (1 + √2)n.

HistoryLogarithms

The history of logarithms

Find out why logarithms were developed and how they made difficult calculations more manageable before electronic calculators.

Questions to explore: How do logarithms turn multiplication into addition? What problem was John Napier trying to solve? How did logarithm tables and the slide rule change scientific work?

Possible connection: Compare a calculation done by hand with a logarithm table, then explain which steps save effort and why.

HistoryLight & Optics

The history and mathematics of Snell's law

Explore how the bending of light when it passes between materials can be described by the relationship n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂.

Questions to explore: How do the angles change when light enters water or glass? Can measurements from a simple refraction experiment support the law? How did ideas about refraction develop before Willebrord Snell's work?

Possible connection: Investigate how Fermat's principle of least time gives another way to understand why light changes direction.
